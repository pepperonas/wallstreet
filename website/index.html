Terminal · Open source · Free

The trading floor,
in your terminal.

wallstreet turns one terminal window into a trading-desk screen: live quotes, a chart for the stock you click, three tickers for gainers, losers and headlines, a feed reader and press photos as ASCII art. No API key, no account — and a demo mode when the news should not be real.

Real numbers. Real news. One window.

Click a stock, see its chart

The table lists the most active stocks, the Nasdaq-100 movers, advancers and decliners. Click a row or move with the arrow keys, and the pane beside it draws that stock's course: today from the opening bell, or a month, three months, the year so far or a full year.

Three tickers

Across the bottom of the window: the day's biggest gainers in green, the biggest losers in red and the headlines of all feeds in yellow, each scrolling at its own pace.

News beside the numbers

A feed reader shows the latest articles and reloads by itself; next to it a press photo from the feeds appears as coloured ASCII art. One list of feeds drives the reader, the ticker and the pictures — edit it to follow other news.

No key, no account

The quotes come from the Nasdaq market-movers interface, the news from public RSS feeds of BBC, CNBC, MarketWatch and the WSJ. Nothing to register, nothing to configure, and wallstreet itself collects nothing.

A demo mode for the camera

wallstreet --demo shows twenty companies that do not exist, prices that drift every minute and invented headlines. Nothing is fetched — for presentations, screenshots, a film set or a machine without a network.

Careful with what the network sends

Headlines and company names are stripped of control characters before they reach your terminal. Pictures are loaded over http(s) from public hosts only, and the cache is a folder that only you can write to.

What it looks like

Install in three steps

  1. 1

    Install

    With Homebrew: brew install pepperonas/tap/wallstreet brings wallstreet and the programs its panes use. Without Homebrew, curl -fsSL https://github.com/pepperonas/wallstreet/releases/latest/download/install.sh | sh puts it into ~/.local and names what is missing.

  2. 2

    Run

    Type wallstreet in a terminal — the wider the window, the more panes. Click a stock to see its chart. wallstreet --demo shows invented data instead of the day's news.

  3. 3

    Quit

    Press Ctrl-C in any pane. The window closes, the tmux session that wallstreet started ends, and you are back at your prompt.

Questions

Is wallstreet free?

Yes. It is open source under the Apache-2.0 licence, without ads, accounts or telemetry, and it needs no paid data subscription. The original was written by Dustin Kirkland as part of hollywood.

Where do the numbers come from?

From the market-movers interface of nasdaq.com: the most active, rising and falling stocks of the trading day. Outside trading hours the last close stays on screen. They are for looking at, not for trading.

What does it need?

macOS or Linux, tmux and Python 3.9 or newer. The feed reader needs newsboat, the picture img2txt from libcaca; without them those two panes stay away. Homebrew installs all of it.

How do I update?

With Homebrew: brew upgrade wallstreet. Otherwise run the installer script again; it replaces the installed copy with the newest release.

Can I follow my own stocks or other news?

Other news: yes — the file newsboat.urls lists the feeds, one per line. Your own stocks: no. The table shows the day's market movers; a watch list is not part of wallstreet.

Is the download genuine?

Every release is built by GitHub Actions from the tagged source. This page shows the SHA-256 of each file; compare it with shasum -a 256, or let the installer script do it.

Like the app?

wallstreet is free, has no ads and stays that way. A small donation keeps it maintained — and a review of celox.io on Google helps just as much.
